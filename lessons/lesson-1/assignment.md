## Part 2: Follow the Stack

### Purpose

An open-source project can become the center of an industry ecosystem. Companies may build products and hosted services around it; other projects may extend or integrate with it; employers may hire people specifically to operate or develop it.

The original version of this assignment focused only on CNCF projects. Modern software and AI systems span several layers, so you may investigate a relevant project from any open-source ecosystem.

### Choose a project

Choose one open-source project involved in building, deploying, or operating modern software or AI systems.

Possible starting points include:

- **Infrastructure and operations:** Kubernetes, OpenTelemetry, Envoy, Prometheus, or Backstage
- **AI frameworks and inference:** PyTorch, vLLM, Ray, or DeepSpeed
- **Applications and agents:** OpenClaw or another instructor-approved open-source project

You may choose a different project if you can identify its official repository and open-source license.

### Submit

Write approximately 350–500 words that answer the following questions:

1. **What is the project?** Link its official repository and explain the problem it solves in your own words.
2. **Is it open source?** Identify its license and link the license file or official license page.
3. **Who stewards it?** Identify its foundation, company, or independent governance community. If the answer is unclear, describe what you checked.
4. **What evidence shows an ecosystem around it?** Identify two concrete products, hosted services, integrations, standards, companies, or job categories that depend on or build around the project.
5. **What is one current activity signal?** Examples include a recent release, recent commit, active issue discussion, project roadmap, or contributor meeting.
6. **What is your conclusion?** In two or three sentences, explain how this project has enabled technical or economic activity beyond its own repository.

Include at least three supporting links. At least one must be an official project, repository, foundation, or documentation source.

### What counts as strong evidence?

- A vendor's documentation shows that its product integrates with or is built on the project.
- A foundation or project page identifies an official integration, adopter, or hosted service.
- Job postings consistently ask for experience with the project.
- Multiple independent projects extend a documented API, plugin system, or standard.

Stars, forks, contributor counts, and recent commits are useful signals, but none of them alone proves that a project created an industry.

### Credit

Full credit requires:

- a complete response to all six questions;
- specific claims supported by working links;
- a clear distinction between repository activity and broader industry impact; and
- an explanation written primarily in your own words.

We are evaluating investigation and effort, not marketing polish or prior expertise.
